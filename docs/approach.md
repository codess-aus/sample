# Approach

These workshops are not lectures. They are not product demonstrations. They are
not a series of slides with AI buzzwords on them.

They are structured learning experiences, built on years of teaching, mentoring and
working inside real organisations — designed to help people think more clearly and
act more effectively.

---

## Practical Rather Than Theoretical

Every concept introduced in these workshops is connected to a real scenario,
a genuine decision or an actual challenge that organisations and individuals face.

Abstract theory has its place. But workshops for leaders and students work best when
the learning is grounded in something that feels real — because it is.

!!! info "Experience over abstraction"
    These workshops draw on experience designing nationally accredited qualifications,
    mentoring professionals across industry, and working at the intersection of
    academia and applied technology.

---

## Based on Real Industry Experience

The content of these workshops is not sourced from textbooks or vendor documentation.
It comes from years of working with organisations across sectors, observing what
actually works, what fails in practice, and what questions people do not know to ask
until things go wrong.

That experience shapes everything: the examples used, the problems explored, the
risks highlighted and the frameworks applied.

---

## Interactive and Discussion-Led

Participants are active contributors to their own learning — not passive recipients
of information.

Every session includes opportunities for:

- Discussion and debate
- Collaborative problem-solving
- Challenging assumptions — including the facilitator's
- Applying frameworks to real scenarios
- Sharing experience and perspective from within the room

The facilitator asks more questions than they answer.

## Mentoring Techniques Used in These Workshops

Mentoring is most effective when it is deliberate, structured and adapted to the
person in front of you. The techniques below are used throughout these workshops
to help participants make meaningful progress, not just feel temporarily inspired.

### 1) Socratic Questioning for Better Product Judgement

Instead of giving immediate answers, mentors use targeted questions to improve
how mentees reason through ambiguity:

- What user problem are you really solving?
- What evidence supports this priority?
- What trade-off are you accepting by choosing this path?
- What would make this recommendation fail?

This develops independent product thinking, which is essential when no one can
give you a perfect answer in advance.

### 2) Goal Laddering from Long-Term Vision to Weekly Action

Many mentees set vague goals such as "become a better PM." Goal laddering turns
that into actionable progression:

- Top rung: long-term direction (for example, become a PM trusted to lead strategy)
- Middle rungs: capability themes (discovery, prioritisation, stakeholder influence)
- Bottom rungs: weekly behaviours (run one user interview, write one PRD section,
  facilitate one decision meeting)

This method improves momentum because mentees can see exactly what to do next.

### 3) Decision Journaling to Improve Product Instinct Over Time

Mentees record important product decisions with four elements:

1. Context and constraints
2. Options considered
3. Chosen path and reasoning
4. Predicted outcomes and confidence level

When revisited later, this creates a high-quality learning loop. Mentees see where
their judgement was strong, where assumptions were weak and how to calibrate future
decisions with greater precision.

### 4) Assumption Mapping Before Roadmap Commitment

Product plans often fail because hidden assumptions are treated as facts. Mentors
teach mentees to map assumptions by:

- User behaviour assumptions
- Technical feasibility assumptions
- Business impact assumptions
- Organisational dependency assumptions

Each assumption is then tested in the lowest-cost way possible before major
investment.

### 5) Live Stakeholder Simulation

Mentees practice difficult conversations in realistic role-play scenarios:

- Pushback from engineering on scope
- Sales pressure for custom features
- Executive requests without supporting evidence
- Conflicting priorities across teams

Simulation builds confidence and communication agility before real meetings where
stakes are higher.

### 6) Feedback in Two Speeds: Immediate and Reflective

Mentors provide:

- Immediate feedback on specific behaviours in the moment
- Reflective feedback later on recurring patterns and growth priorities

This combination helps mentees improve execution now while still developing deeper
professional maturity over time.

## Ten Tips for Mentees to Succeed in Product Management

1. **Start with the problem, not the feature.**
  Define the user pain, business relevance and current workaround before proposing
  a solution.

2. **Write clearly and often.**
  Strong product managers create alignment through concise writing: problem briefs,
  decision notes, priorities and trade-offs.

3. **Practice structured discovery every week.**
  Speak with users regularly, not occasionally. Consistency in discovery beats
  occasional deep dives.

4. **Learn to prioritise with explicit criteria.**
  Use transparent criteria such as impact, effort, risk and strategic fit so
  stakeholders understand why some work is sequenced later.

5. **Treat assumptions as testable hypotheses.**
  Convert opinions into experiments. Small tests reduce expensive mistakes.

6. **Build strong relationships with engineering and design early.**
  Product success is cross-functional. Trust and shared context produce better
  outcomes than handoffs.

7. **Communicate trade-offs before committing timelines.**
  Every roadmap choice has an opportunity cost. Make those costs visible early.

8. **Seek feedback before your work is polished.**
  Early feedback on rough drafts of strategy, requirements and metrics saves time
  and improves quality.

9. **Measure outcomes, not output.**
  Shipping features is not the goal. Changed user behaviour and measurable business
  improvement are the goal.

10. **Run your own growth system.**
   Keep a learning log, track recurring mistakes, choose one improvement focus each
   month and review progress with your mentor.

These ten habits, applied consistently, create the foundations of strong product
management: better judgement, clearer communication, stronger collaboration and
measurable impact.

## Tailored Guidance by Product Management Level

The same advice does not work equally well at every stage. Mentoring is most
effective when expectations, practice and feedback are matched to career level.

### Entry-Level PM Mentees

At this stage, success comes from building reliable fundamentals and learning to
deliver consistently in a team environment.

- **Primary focus:** problem definition, user interviews, clear written updates
- **Key habit:** produce one short weekly artefact (for example, a one-page problem
  brief, interview summary or decision note)
- **Common risk:** jumping to solutions too quickly to appear "strategic"
- **Mentor intervention:** insist on evidence before solutioning and coach concise
  communication to reduce ambiguity

### Associate PM Mentees

At associate level, mentees need to move from execution support to ownership of
small product areas and measurable outcomes.

- **Primary focus:** prioritisation, cross-functional alignment, scope management
- **Key habit:** run fortnightly trade-off reviews with engineering and design
- **Common risk:** overcommitting on timelines to satisfy every stakeholder
- **Mentor intervention:** teach structured prioritisation and explicit opportunity
  cost communication

### Early-Career PMs in Technical Products

For PMs working with data, AI or platform-heavy products, success depends on
translating between technical complexity and customer value.

- **Primary focus:** system constraints, model limitations, reliability and risk
- **Key habit:** write requirement statements that separate user outcomes from
  implementation assumptions
- **Common risk:** relying on technical novelty without clear user impact
- **Mentor intervention:** enforce outcome metrics, assumption testing and strong
  partnering with technical leads

### 30-60-90 Day Mentoring Roadmap

To convert advice into progress, mentees can use a simple progression plan:

- **First 30 days:** observe and document decisions, stakeholder dynamics and user
  pain patterns
- **By 60 days:** own a scoped problem area with clear success metrics
- **By 90 days:** lead one cross-functional decision from discovery through delivery
  and retrospective

This level-specific structure helps mentees develop confidence and credibility
without skipping core capability-building steps.

## Inclusive and Accessible

Good education should not require people to already know the answer before they
can engage with the material.

These workshops are designed to be accessible to participants who are new to AI,
to data science and to formal problem-solving methods. No jargon is used without
explanation. No assumption is made that participants have a technical background.

At the same time, the content is substantive enough to engage participants who do
have technical backgrounds — because the skills being developed go beyond any
single discipline.

---

## Adaptable to Different Audiences

The same core ideas are relevant to a board-level executive and to a high school
student — but the framing, examples and activities need to be different.

Before every engagement, a brief discovery conversation ensures the workshop is
designed around the specific audience: their background, their current level of
understanding, their challenges and their goals.

The result is a session that feels tailored, not generic.

---

## Focused on Understanding, Not Product Promotion

These workshops have no commercial relationship with any AI vendor, platform or
tool provider. No particular product is promoted. No affiliate arrangement
influences the content.

The goal is to develop understanding that is durable across whatever tools exist
today and whatever tools will exist in the future.

---

## Designed to Create Lasting Capability

The measure of a good workshop is not what participants say at the end. It is what
they are able to do differently three months later.

These workshops aim to leave participants with:

- Frameworks they can apply to new situations
- Questions they know how to ask
- Confidence to engage with AI — and AI vendors — on their own terms
- A habit of thinking clearly before acting quickly

That is the kind of capability worth building.
